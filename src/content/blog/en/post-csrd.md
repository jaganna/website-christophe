## 1 - A strengthening of the regulatory framework for sustainability reporting

###### Directive (EU) 2022/2464, known as the CSRD, constitutes the European Union's legal framework for sustainability reporting. It fundamentally reforms companies' disclosure obligations by strengthening and expanding the information to be provided on environmental, social, and governance (ESG) issues, in order to improve its availability and comparability for stakeholders.

## 2 - The integration of sustainability reporting into financial reporting and the introduction of insurance

###### The directive requires that sustainability information be integrated into the management report, in a clearly identifiable section, to strengthen the consistency between financial and non-financial data. It also provides for a phased assurance mechanism: sustainability reporting must initially be subject to limited assurance, with the possibility of moving towards reasonable assurance once European standards are fully established. The assurance opinion must, in particular, verify compliance with European requirements, reporting standards, the process for identifying published information, and tagging rules.

## 3 - Standardization and digitalization of sustainability information 

###### The CSRD also emphasizes the digital dimension: reports must be prepared in the European electronic format, and sustainability information must be tagged to facilitate its use and comparability. In this context, the European Sustainability Reporting Standards (ESRS) play a central role by specifying the content and structure of the information to be published, thus ensuring the practical implementation of the directive's requirements.
